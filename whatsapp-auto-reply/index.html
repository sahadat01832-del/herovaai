Automatic WhatsApp replies for a small business: what actually works

Last updated 9 October 2026

Most small businesses set up an automatic reply expecting to stop answering the same question all day, and then discover the reply goes out but the customer still asks again. The reason is simple: an away message is a notice, not an answer. This page separates the two — what WhatsApp already does for free, where it stops being enough, and what to check before you automate anything.

The short version: free canned messages handle timing (“we're closed, we'll reply at 9”). They cannot handle the questions that decide whether someone buys — “how much is it”, “do you have it in blue”, “can I get it today”. Those need your actual prices, stock and hours, which is where an assistant that knows your business starts to earn its place.

1. What WhatsApp gives you for free

Before paying for anything, use what is already included in the WhatsApp Business app:

  • Away message. An automatic reply outside your business hours. Good for setting expectations, useless for informing anyone.
  • Greeting message. Sent the first time a customer messages you, so nobody stares at silence while they decide whether you are still in business.
  • Quick replies. Shortcuts you type yourself — /price, /hours — that expand into a saved reply. They save typing, but you still have to be at the phone.
  • Labels and catalog. Organising chats and listing products so a customer can browse without asking.

If your only problem is “customers message at midnight”, the away message already solves it. Stop there; it is free and it is honest.

2. Where canned replies break

They break on the questions that repeat with small variations, because a saved reply is a fixed string. A real conversation looks like this:

  • “How much for two dozen cupcakes delivered tomorrow?” — a price, a quantity, and a date in one sentence. No canned reply contains that.
  • “Do you still have the blue one?” — depends on today's stock, not on anything you wrote down in advance.
  • “Is my order ready?” — needs the specific order, not your opening hours.
  • “Same as last time, please.” — needs a memory of the last time.

Businesses usually respond to this by writing more canned replies. Twenty saved messages later, the customer has been answered with something slightly wrong, and now thinks nobody read their message — which is worse than the original silence.

3. What an assistant adds

An AI assistant answering on WhatsApp changes one thing: it writes the reply at the moment the question arrives, from the business facts you gave it, instead of choosing between pre-written strings.

  • It answers from your data. Your prices, your hours, your delivery rules, your catalog — the same facts, rephrased for whatever the customer actually asked.
  • It remembers context. A second question in the same chat is understood as a follow-up, and a returning customer can be recognised.
  • It answers when you cannot. The 21:00 message gets a real answer instead of a notice, while you are serving the customer in front of you.
  • It can hand over. When a question falls outside what it knows, the sensible behaviour is to say so and flag the chat for a human — not to guess.

4. A checklist before you automate

  1. Write down the facts. Prices, hours, delivery areas, lead times, what you refuse to do. Without this, any assistant will improvise — and you will not like the result.
  2. List the ten questions you actually get. These are the ones worth automating; everything else is decoration.
  3. Decide the boundaries. What must it never say — invented prices, invented availability, medical or legal advice, promises about a specific delivery time?
  4. Keep a human path. There has to be an obvious way for a confused customer to reach you.
  5. Review the first week's chats. Read what it said, correct the business facts, then read again. The corrections are what make it sound like you.
  6. Check the privacy story. Where do the conversations live, who can read them, and which AI provider sees the text? This should be written down, not assumed.

5. Effort and cost, honestly

Automating replies is not zero-work. Expect to spend an afternoon writing down your business facts, and a little time each week at the start reviewing replies. Cloud AI models bill per message and the cost tracks how much people message you; running a model on your own machine avoids per-message bills but needs a machine that stays on, and its answers will be less fluent. Any honest setup tells you which model answered, so a wrong price can be traced to a cause rather than a mystery.
